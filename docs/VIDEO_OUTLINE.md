# Demo video outline — "Are You Asking the Right Questions?"
## Brian's structure: broad bold vision first, then ONE specific problem.
## Target 2–3 minutes. The video is scored (inside the 25% Demo slice).

### Part 1 — BROAD (about 0:00–0:40): it all starts with the right questions
- Rapid, warm montage in words (Brian on camera or voiceover over simple
  visuals): a detective works a case. A lawyer questions a witness. A
  company sends a survey. A salesperson opens a call. A person types a
  prompt into a large language model.
- The Danvers beat, played with humor: in *True Detective: Night Country*,
  Jodie Foster's Chief Liz Danvers keeps telling her team the same thing —
  "We are just not seeing it. Not asking the right questions." A fictional
  detective solves crimes by asking the right questions. So does everyone
  above. The question comes first; the answer can only be as good as it.
- The turn: "So who checks the questions when a MACHINE writes them?"

### Part 2 — THE SINGLE PROBLEM (about 0:40–1:10)
- One problem, stated once, plainly: AI systems now write prediction-market
  and research questions at a scale no team can check by hand. (Forecast's
  team moderated every question by hand; that is the job that disappeared.)
  A tilted question quietly rigs every answer that follows — and nobody
  independent is checking.
- Title lands: "Are You Asking the Right Questions?" — an independent audit
  for AI-generated questions.

### Part 3 — THE DEMO (about 1:10–2:20): one question, live
- Muse Spark writes a forecast question from a trending topic, on screen.
- The audit scores it live: wording flags (one-sided? hidden assumption?),
  the order check (answers in both orders), settleability (named source?
  named date? one clear outcome?).
- The question FAILS on something real. Spark rewrites it using only the
  flag reasons. The engine re-scores: before → after, on screen.
- One line of credibility, no more: the engine already ran on 100 real
  AI-generated research questions; about 1 in 8 were one-sided; an
  independent re-scoring agreed 95 times out of 100.

### Part 4 — CLOSE (about 2:20–2:45): back to broad, one breath
- "Their models write the market, play the market, and fix the market.
  The referee is the only thing in the room that isn't theirs."
- Final card: "It all starts with the right questions."

  the repo README, not the video — the video stays clean.)
